# Standard Report Sections

Every client report contains exactly these 14 sections, in this order, with these IDs and eyebrow labels. Do not add, remove, or reorder sections.

Every section **must use the section-bar heading pattern** from `components.md` instead of a plain eyebrow + h2. The section-bar renders a full-width black bar that breaks out of the container padding, with the eyebrow label in red above a white h2.

**Every section must also end with a `sources` block** (see `components.md`) listing the 2–5 specific sources used for that section, each with a publication date in `(Mon YYYY)` form. Sources should be less than 1 year old wherever possible, and no older than 2 years except where no recent source exists. This applies to all 14 sections.

**Sources must be independent, not just the client's own website.** The client's site is evidence of what the client *says*; every section needs at least one third-party source (regulator, filing, press, analyst/data tool, vendor, platform), and client-owned sources may make up no more than half of any section. See `research-guide.md` → *Source independence* for what to use where.

## s01 — Company Overview
**Eyebrow:** `Client Background`
**h2:** `Company Overview.`

Cover: legal name, HQ, parent company / ownership structure, founding year, number of employees, core business description, and CEO / key leadership. Use a 4-card feature-grid for the quick-reference facts (HQ, ownership, workforce, CEO), followed by a note callout for the most strategically relevant context.

---

## s02 — Products & Services
**Eyebrow:** `Portfolio`
**h2:** `Products &amp; Services.`
*(for brand-heavy clients substitute `Brand Architecture` as eyebrow and `Brand Portfolio.` as h2)*

Cover: what the company sells or makes, product lines or service categories, sub-brands or divisions, and any recent portfolio changes. Use step blocks (one per major product/brand/line) for depth. Add a note callout if there is a significant portfolio transition underway.

---

## s03 — Financial Performance
**Eyebrow:** `Financial Snapshot`
**h2:** `Financial Performance.`

Cover: most recent full-year revenue and profit figures, year-on-year trend, key margin or volume metrics, and any notable one-off events (acquisitions, write-downs, tariff impacts). Use a 4-card feature-grid for the headline numbers. Add a color-legend if categorising different revenue streams. Close with a note callout on headwinds or tailwinds.

---

## s04 — Digital Transformation & Technology
**Eyebrow:** `Digital &amp; Technology`
**h2:** `Digital Transformation &amp; Technology.`

Cover: current tech stack (CMS, commerce platform, analytics tools, data infrastructure), digital initiatives in flight, e-commerce presence, app or mobile strategy, and any major technology investments or partnerships. Use step blocks numbered by initiative priority (number in the title, e.g. `1. Initiative.`). Add a note callout listing known domains and tech partners.

---

## s05 — Marketing & Advertising Strategy
**Eyebrow:** `Marketing &amp; Communications`
**h2:** `Marketing &amp; Advertising Strategy.`

Cover: brand positioning and messaging, campaign philosophy, channel mix (paid, owned, earned), agency relationships, notable recent campaigns, and any known budget signals. Use a 4-card feature-grid to contrast positioning across brands or channels. Add a note callout flagging any agency review or procurement activity relevant to Adobe.

---

## s06 — Social Media & Content Strategy
**Eyebrow:** `Social &amp; Content`
**h2:** `Social Media &amp; Content Strategy.`

Cover: primary social platforms, follower scale and engagement benchmarks, content cadence and format mix (video, static, UGC, live), influencer or creator programmes, and any evidence of social commerce. Use step blocks (one per primary platform). Add a note callout on content production bottlenecks or creator spend signals.

---

## s07 — SEO & Search Visibility
**Eyebrow:** `Search &amp; Discovery`
**h2:** `SEO &amp; Search Visibility.`

Cover: organic search performance (estimated traffic, domain authority, top-ranking categories), paid search presence, any known SEO tooling or agency, evidence of AI-search / LLM visibility concerns, and key keyword territories. Use step blocks for each search channel or topic cluster. Add a note callout on any gap between paid and organic that represents an Adobe opportunity.

---

## s08 — Brand & Website Portfolio
**Eyebrow:** `Brand &amp; Web Presence`
**h2:** `Brand &amp; Website Portfolio.`

Cover: all public-facing web domains and microsites, the CMS or platform behind each, localisation breadth (languages, markets), accessibility posture, and any evidence of site fragmentation or consolidation plans. Use step blocks (one per major domain or brand cluster). Add a note callout if site sprawl or inconsistent tech is a likely pain point.

---

## s09 — Competitive Landscape
**Eyebrow:** `Competitive Intelligence`
**h2:** `Competitive Landscape.`

Cover: top 3–5 direct competitors, relative positioning (price, quality, market share), recent competitive moves (new products, marketing spend, acquisitions), and any identified gaps the client is trying to close. Use a 4-card feature-grid to compare the client against its top two or three competitors on the axes most relevant to Adobe's pitch. Add a note callout on competitive pressure that creates urgency for digital investment.

---

## s10 — Customer & Market Profile
**Eyebrow:** `Audience &amp; Markets`
**h2:** `Customer &amp; Market Profile.`

Cover: primary customer demographics and psychographics, geographic market breakdown, top markets by revenue or growth, D2C vs. retail/partner channel split, and any audience shifts underway. Do not repeat competitor positioning — that belongs in s09. Use a 4-card feature-grid (e.g. primary buyer / secondary buyer / key markets / go-to-market approach). Add a note callout on any significant audience transition.

---

## s11 — Regulatory & Compliance
**Eyebrow:** `Regulatory Environment`
**h2:** `Regulatory &amp; Compliance.`

Cover: key regulations affecting the client's markets (data privacy, consumer protection, sector-specific rules), current compliance posture, any active investigations or fines, and how regulatory requirements shape their data, marketing, or technology decisions. Use step blocks (one per major regulatory domain). Add a note callout on any regulatory driver that accelerates a need for Adobe's data or consent management products.

---

## s12 — Stakeholder Mapping
**Eyebrow:** `Key Stakeholders`
**h2:** `Stakeholder Mapping.`

Cover: decision-makers relevant to Adobe (CMO, CTO, CDO, CPO, CFO), their reported priorities or public statements, known internal tensions (marketing vs. IT, brand vs. performance), and any recent leadership changes that shift buying dynamics. Use step blocks (one per key stakeholder or persona). Add a note callout on the most likely internal champion and the most likely blocker.

---

## s13 — Strategic Direction
**Eyebrow:** `Strategic Direction`
**h2:** `Strategic Direction.`
*(substitute a named strategy if one is public, e.g. `Reimagine Strategy &amp; Electrification.`)*

Cover: publicly stated multi-year strategy, transformation programme, M&A activity, sustainability commitments, geographic expansion, and investment priorities. Use numbered step blocks for each strategic pillar (number in the title, e.g. `1. Pillar.`). Add a color-legend if categorising short / medium / long-term horizons.

---

## s14 — Adobe Opportunity
**Eyebrow:** `Adobe Alignment`
**h2:** `Adobe Opportunity.`

Cover: 4–6 specific Adobe products with a concrete business case for each, tied directly to a pain point or initiative identified in the earlier sections. Open with a short paragraph stating how the recommendations are ranked (deal-size potential, timing, who owns the budget). Pick products and use their **current names** from `workflow/adobe-products.md`. Use numbered step blocks, one per product, with the title written as **`1. Adobe Product (detail) — the headline benefit`** (for example `1. Adobe Real-Time CDP (Adobe Experience Platform) — co-sell into payments and banking`).

Each step body is **one flowing paragraph of roughly 100–180 words** in plain prose: the specific client facts and figures that justify it, what the product does about them, any compliance or regulatory angle, and a closing sentence on sequencing or timing. **Do not use labelled sub-parts** such as "Fit:", "Outcome:" or "Timing:" — the reference report (`examples/example report.html`) shows the required style. Where a fact comes from an earlier section, name that section and link it (see *Cross-references* in `design/components.md`) instead of writing a section code. Close with a note callout on timing, urgency, or competitive risk.

**Do not use generic Adobe pitch language. Every recommendation must be grounded in a fact from the earlier sections.**

## Section separator rule

After every section except s14, add `<hr>`. s14 has no trailing `<hr>` — the footer follows immediately.
