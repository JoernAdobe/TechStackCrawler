Client Background

Company Overview.

Endava plc is a London-headquartered technology services group founded in 2000 by its current CEO, John Cotterell, and listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE: DAVA) since 2018. It combines software engineering, industry expertise and what it calls an "AI-native" delivery approach to modernise platforms and build digital products for clients in payments, banking and capital markets, insurance, technology/media/telecoms, mobility, healthcare, and retail and consumer goods. At 31 March 2026 it employed 11,225 people across locations in Europe, the Americas, Asia Pacific and the Middle East, working from more than 25 countries.

Registered investor address: Endava plc, 125 Old Broad Street, London EC2N 1AR. The company describes itself as working from over 25 countries across six continents.

Portfolio

Products & Services.

Endava sells engineering and transformation services rather than products. Its portfolio is organised into core capabilities, a set of branded "Dava" frameworks and programmes, and industry accelerators — most visibly in payments. The company does not publish revenue by service line; it reports revenue by industry vertical and geography only.

Core capabilities

Seven published capability areas: AI; Data; Digital Engineering; Digital Product Acceleration; Advisory and Digital Strategy; Modern Managed Services (cloud applications, infrastructure and workplace operations); and Leading Delivery (agile programme leadership).

Dava.X — specialist focus areas

Split into "Accelerate" (AI, core modernisation, cloud, cyber security, embedded systems) and "Invent" (sustainability, physical computing, quantum). Positioned by CTO Matt Cloke as the route to adopting emerging technologies "rapidly and at scale".

Dava.Flow — AI-native engagement system

Endava's delivery methodology built on a "Change Delivery Lifecycle" of four phases (Signal, Explore, Govern, Evolve), with policy-in-pipeline governance, telemetry and human review of key decisions. Endava claims it typically reduces time-to-market by up to 40%. It was used in the Tyl by NatWest payments engagement announced in May 2026.

Payments IP and operator roles

Endava deploys a Payments Gateway Accelerator (used for Tyl by NatWest) and was appointed technical operator for Nexus Global Payments in February 2026. Payments was the single largest vertical at 23% of Q3 FY2026 revenue.

Ecosystem programmes — Dava.Rise and Dava.Alliance

Dava.Rise (launched November 2025) matches scale-ups with enterprise buyers; the first cohort was UK-focused, with industry cohorts planned for 2026. Dava.Alliance is a partner ecosystem whose named founding members include Island Technologies and Access Holdings. Dava.Y and Dava.Studios also appear in site navigation; their scope was not detailed in the material reviewed.

Portfolio transition: Endava reports that AI-driven work rose from 5% of revenue in Q3 FY2025 to 15% in Q3 FY2026, while the revenue mix moved towards payments (19% to 23% year on year) and away from TMT (18% to 16%).

Financial Snapshot

Financial Performance.

Endava's fiscal year ends 30 June. FY2025 was a year of modest growth: revenue rose 4.3% to £772.3m (6.3% at constant currency), profit before tax was £24.1m and adjusted PBT £82.1m (10.6% margin). FY2026 has gone into reverse. Nine-month revenue fell 7.6% to £540.8m, and Q3 (to 31 March 2026) revenue fell 8.4% to £178.5m, with adjusted PBT down to £3.2m (1.8% margin) from £24.6m (12.6%). Q3 also carried a £364.6m non-cash goodwill impairment, following a reforecast of performance through FY2031, and a £23.2m charge to write off the entire UK deferred tax asset. At 31 March 2026 cash stood at £48.4m against £195.8m of borrowings. FY2026 full-year results and the 20-F are now due "by 2 November 2026", following the accounting investigation.

Digital & Technology

Digital Transformation & Technology.

Endava's technology agenda has two layers. Its delivery business is being rebuilt around AI and agentic tooling through a steady run of partnerships. Its own go-to-market estate, by contrast, runs on marketing-led SaaS: live detection of endava.com in September 2026 showed HubSpot CMS rather than an enterprise DXP. Initiatives are listed below in order of strategic weight.

1. AI-native delivery at scale

Dava.Flow is now the default engagement model. Endava said in September 2025 that more than half of its people use AI in projects, and AI-driven work reached 15% of Q3 FY2026 revenue. The company has also partnered with OpenAI to embed generative models in client work.

2. Agentic engineering and AI transformation partners

Endava expanded its Cognition partnership to scale "governed agentic coding" in February 2026. The same month it partnered with Miro on a new AI-transformation engagement model.

3. Cloud, data and security foundations

Endava partnered with Wiz (now part of Google Cloud) for integrated cloud security for enterprise AI adoption in June 2026. Its standing cloud and data partners include AWS, Microsoft, Google Cloud (Premier Partner), Databricks and Snowflake.

In July 2026 Endava became launch partner for the Upstream Works AI Experience Platform, an agentic, omnichannel contact-centre orchestration layer due to be available in Fall 2026. It targets travel, hospitality, financial services and retail.

Marketing & Communications

Endava markets like a B2B professional-services firm. It relies on owned content, research reports, partner announcements and case studies rather than paid brand advertising. The brand line is "Technology is our how. And people are our why.", and the core message is an "AI-native approach to digital transformation" that helps clients "shape the future with intelligence". Partnership news has run at roughly one to three announcements a month through 2026: Cognition, Miro and Nexus Global Payments in February; NatWest and Mastercard in May; Wiz in June; and Upstream Works in July.

Positioning

AI-native transformation partner

This combines "engineering and industry expertise", "AI-native delivery" and "core modernisation capabilities", backed by 25 years of transformation experience.

Proof engine

Topic clusters

The content concentrates on AI-native and agentic delivery, core modernisation, payments and banking, and industry-specific transformation. This matches the revenue weighting towards payments and banking and capital markets (45% of Q3 FY2026).

International search

AI-search readiness

Branded search reputation

Opportunity gap: Endava sells "AI-native" transformation but does not visibly manage how AI assistants find, read or describe it. It has a large, citation-worthy content library, yet no llms.txt, no AI-crawler policy and no localisation. Meanwhile, third-party negative content is shaping its brand queries right now.

Competitive Intelligence

Competitive Landscape.

Endava's own FY2025 20-F names four groups of competitors. The first is next-generation digital IT service providers (Globant, EPAM Systems, Grid Dynamics, Thoughtworks). The second is digital agencies and consultancies (McKinsey & Company, IDEO, Publicis Sapient). The third is global consulting and IT services firms (Accenture, Capgemini, Cognizant, TCS). The fourth is clients' own in-house development teams, a threat the company says AI is sharpening.

Client

Endava — contracting

Q3 FY2026 revenue fell 8.4% to £178.5m, with an adjusted PBT margin of 1.8% and 11,225 staff. Its differentiators are UK and payments depth, Dava.Flow, and nearshore delivery.

Closest peer

Q2 2026 revenue was $1.415bn (+4.5%), with the full-year growth guide raised to 3.2%–4.2%. It has about 62,850 staff and a non-GAAP operating margin of 16.4%. It also positions itself as an "AI-native" transformation partner.

Peer under pressure

Globant

Global SIs, agencies & in-house teams

Regulatory Environment

Regulatory & Compliance.

Endava faces regulation on two fronts. As a UK-incorporated, NYSE-listed foreign private issuer it is subject to US securities disclosure rules. As a supplier to regulated financial-services, insurance and healthcare clients in the UK and EU, it inherits their operational-resilience, data-protection and AI obligations through contracts.

Securities disclosure and governance

Independent outside counsel for the Audit Committee is investigating concerns about the accounting treatment of certain customer and supplier agreements. The CFO is on administrative leave, and the FY2026 20-F is due by 2 November 2026. US law firms, including Pomerantz (24 September 2026), have announced investigations on behalf of investors. No lawsuit outcome or regulatory finding has been reported.

Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA)

DORA has applied since 17 January 2025 to EU financial entities and their ICT third-party service providers. It covers ICT third-party risk management, key contractual provisions and incident reporting. With 45% of revenue from payments and banking, Endava is exposed as an ICT supplier within clients' third-party frameworks.

EU AI Act

Most remaining provisions have applied since 2 August 2026. Providers of generative systems already on the market must meet Article 50(2) transparency obligations by 2 December 2026, and Annex III high-risk obligations apply from 2 December 2027. This is relevant to the AI and agentic systems Endava builds for credit, insurance and customer-service use cases.

Data protection and consent (UK GDPR / EU GDPR)

endava.com runs a HubSpot cookie banner with accept and reject options, and publishes a privacy notice and cookie policy. Client work in banking and healthcare carries personal-data processing obligations. The CMP vendor beyond HubSpot's native banner was not identified.

Assurance posture and accessibility

Endava holds its fifth consecutive SOC 2 Type II attestation (January 2026) plus ISO 27001, 9001, 14001, 37001, 20000-1 and 22301, Cyber Essentials and TISAX. The European Accessibility Act covers banking services and e-commerce, both core Endava build areas, and the EN 301 549 standard was updated in September 2026.

Regulatory driver: DORA, the AI Act transparency deadlines and accessibility law all push Endava's financial-services clients towards governed data, auditable content and accessible digital experiences. Endava already sells "built-in governance" through Dava.Flow. Platforms with native data-usage labelling, consent enforcement and accessible delivery make that promise easier to keep for regulated clients.

The recommendations are ranked by deal-size potential and timing. The two largest are partner-led co-sell plays through Endava into its financial-services client base. The remaining three are for Endava's own marketing, where near-term budget will be tight under interim finance leadership.

1. Adobe Real-Time CDP (Adobe Experience Platform) — co-sell into payments and banking

Payments and banking and capital markets now make up 45% of Endava's revenue, and its flagship 2026 wins are payments platforms. For Tyl by NatWest, Endava is building a "fully integrated" merchant experience with "data-led insights"; with Mastercard it is co-developing embedded commerce, starting with insurance and healthcare. Its own newsroom credits a payments redesign with 39% conversion growth for Accor Plus, so Endava already sells customer-experience outcomes, not just back-end rebuilds. Real-Time CDP adds a governed customer-profile and activation layer on top of those modernised platforms. Its data-usage labelling and consent enforcement support the DORA and GDPR obligations these clients carry. A joint Endava–Adobe offer turns Endava's "built-in governance" message into something clients can deploy, at no platform cost to Endava.

2. Adobe Experience Manager Sites (Cloud Service with Edge Delivery Services) — win the default DXP slot in Endava's practice

Core modernisation is a headline Dava.X pillar, and Endava's partner wall lists both Sitecore and Adobe. Adobe has no dedicated partner page, while AWS, Microsoft, Google Cloud, Salesforce and OpenAI all do. Packaging AEM Sites modernisation as a Dava.Flow-governed accelerator would give Endava a repeatable offer for its banking, insurance and retail clients. The European Accessibility Act covers banking and e-commerce, so accessible, performant customer-facing sites are now a compliance need as well as a user-experience improvement. A scoped pilot on one Endava client journey, with measurable conversion and speed outcomes, fits the proof-led buying Endava says its clients now demand.

3. Adobe LLM Optimizer — protect how AI assistants describe Endava

Endava's brand queries are currently dominated by law-firm investor alerts, and its site has no llms.txt and no AI-crawler policy. Yet the company sells itself as "AI-native" and publishes 490 insights pages and 157 case studies that could serve as authoritative citations. LLM Optimizer would let the marketing team see how ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and other assistants answer questions about Endava's services and stability, find the gaps, and push its own case studies and partnership facts into those answers. It is a low-cost, fast-to-deploy entry point that does not require replacing HubSpot. It matters most in the run-up to the FY2026 results due by 2 November 2026.

4. Adobe Journey Optimizer B2B Edition — account expansion across a concentrated client base

Endava's top 10 clients generate 40% of revenue, its £1m-plus client count fell from 136 to 129, and growth leadership is split between Financial Services and Commercial Services. HubSpot currently runs web and CTA marketing, and no account-based orchestration was detected. Journey Optimizer B2B Edition is built around buying groups: it could coordinate Endava's research reports, case studies and partner news towards the specific CTO, CIO and product sponsors inside its largest accounts, making account growth a managed process. Position this as a phase-two conversation once FY2026 reporting has stabilised and a permanent CFO is in place.

5. Adobe GenStudio for Performance Marketing — scale content variants without scaling headcount

Endava publishes a high volume of content: 249 articles, 178 podcasts, 157 case studies and partner announcements roughly every month through 2026. It runs regional Instagram accounts for Europe and North America, yet serves everything from a single British-English site while North America provides 38% of revenue. Restructuring and a shrinking headcount mean output has to rise without adding people. GenStudio for Performance Marketing would let a small team turn each report, case study or partner announcement into on-brand regional and channel variants, with brand governance applied before publication. It also gives Endava a working example of its own "AI-native" message.

Timing & urgency: The best entry point is partner-led. Engage Ceri McCall (Group Head of Partnerships) and the two Chief Growth Officers on a joint Real-Time CDP and AEM Sites offer for named financial-services pursuits such as the Tyl by NatWest and Mastercard programmes. Pair it with a low-cost LLM Optimizer engagement for Endava's own brand. The key timing signal is the FY2026 results and 20-F due by 2 November 2026. Once they are out, spending decisions will reopen under clearer governance, and the EU AI Act Article 50 transparency deadline of 2 December 2026 raises demand for governed AI experiences among Endava's clients. There is competitive urgency too: Sitecore sits on the same partner wall, and EPAM is growing on an identical "AI-native" message. Adobe should secure preferred-DXP status in Endava's practice before the next wave of modernisation pursuits. Expect close procurement scrutiny under the interim CFO. Do not use the accounting investigation as a pressure tactic, and do not treat it as a finding.
